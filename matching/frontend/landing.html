i. The problem

Time gets written down anywhere.

A notebook page. A sticky note. A voice memo from the car. An email sent at 11:48 at night.Before any of it gets billed, someone has to sort it all by hand. Usually on a Friday afternoon.

ii. Read

Tenth reads every one.

Handwriting, voice memos, emails, spreadsheets. They all come out as the same clean entries: who, when, how long and what was done.Coffee ring over the hours? Tenth asks instead of guessing.

iii. Match

Then each entry finds its matter.

Every hour bills to a matter: the case, deal or project it was for.Tenth weighs the matter number, the work described and what each person usually works on. Then it picks one and tells you why, in plain words.

iv. Check

Then it flags what a client would push back on.

Vague descriptions get caught before a client ever sees them.Tenth spells out shorthand and fills in details it can find. What it can’t know, like who was on the call, it leaves for you.

Good questions.

Why is it called Tenth?

hover meX, as in tenLawyers bill in tenths of an hour, so 0.1 is six minutes. Six minutes sounds small until you count them across a whole firm for a year.Tenth makes sure every one gets billed.The logo is the Roman numeral for ten, an X with a bar on top and bottom. It doubles as an hourglass.

What can I give it?

Photos of handwritten pages and sticky notes, voice memos, PDFs, Word documents, spreadsheets, screenshots, and emails with attachments.Drop in a whole week at once. Napkins welcome.

What does it check in a description?

Whether each task says what was done, with whom, and about what. That’s what clients and fee examiners look for.Tenth spells out shorthand, splits lumped tasks apart, and fills in missing details it can find. If it can’t find one, it leaves a blank for you instead of guessing.

Does it connect to our billing system?

Not yet.Today you get a spreadsheet (CSV or Excel) to import, with anything still open marked, or a PDF to look over. Connecting straight to billing systems is next.

Was real client data used?

No. Law firms can’t share their clients’ billing records, for privacy reasons.So we built and tested Tenth on a fictional firm, and on time entries already public in court filings.

Is it ready for my firm?

It’s a working first version. Think of it as a very eager new associate.Next up: your firm’s own billing rules, and learning from your corrections.
